Creating a Personalized AI Writing Partner

A writing partner is a set of standing instructions plus a small library of your own work, so the feedback you get is about your writing rather than writing in general. This is the do-it-yourself version of the workshop: why you would build one, what to be careful about, and the five steps.

The basics

How you make one

Several platforms let you save a set of standing instructions that behaves like a small custom AI. ChatGPT has custom GPTs and Claude has Projects, but both of those need a paid account. The session used Gemini Gems, which are available at no cost to anyone with a Google Workspace account — which is why the walkthrough is reproducible for everybody.

Why bother

A partner you have configured is available whenever you are, and it reads a long document far faster than a person can. It is not a substitute for a human reader; it is something you can use at eleven at night on a draft nobody else has time for.

What it can actually do

  • Review a long document in a few minutes
  • Identify issues affecting clarity, consistency, and persuasiveness
  • Give feedback on the specific things you told it to watch for

Ethics, before you build anything

Policy questions to answer first

  • Coursework: does the class policy permit AI use?
  • Publications: what is the journal’s or publisher’s policy?
  • Clinic work: do your supervisors and your clients permit it?
  • Any legal work: does this involve confidential or sensitive information?

Three cautions

  • Hallucinations: check the accuracy of every suggested change.
  • Copyright: being litigated now, and the outcome may affect what can lawfully be used to train these tools.
  • Plagiarism: AI offers existing writing and ideas without attributing them.

The roadmap

1. Gather precedents

Collect a representative sample of your own previous writing — the work you would want new drafts to resemble.

2. Create feedback checklists

Run those samples through a few different models and have each produce a checklist of the recurring problems and things to watch for.

3. Form a knowledge base

Collect those checklists together with any other feedback documents or style guidance you rely on.

4. Write the instructions

Decide what the partner is for: the scope of feedback you want, and the form you want it in.

5. Build the Gem

Attach the knowledge base, paste in the instructions, then test it on a draft you already have opinions about.

Where to go next

  • The Stanford AI Playground — try the same instructions against several models before you commit to one.
  • Build a Writing Partner agent in ChatGPT — the hub’s longer guide, which loads ten library-built writing skills into a single agent, plus the skill files themselves if you would rather not build from scratch.

Privacy and confidentiality. Check how a tool treats your data before you upload your own writing to it — and remember that a writing sample can be as sensitive as anything else you handle. If you would not want it on the front page of the newspaper, do not upload it.
