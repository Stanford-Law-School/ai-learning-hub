Session Two: Breakout Room Lightning Workshops

Seven rooms, forty-five minutes each, one AI workflow per room. Every room’s instructions, files, and trained chat are below, so any of them can be run again — alone, or with a few colleagues and somebody keeping time.

Presented
Wednesday, 20 August 2025, 11:30am PT, on Zoom
Facilitators
Taryn Marks, Will Huggins, Sergio Stone, Kay Cadena, Heather Joy, Grace Lo, Qi Huang, and Nhatrang Nguyen — one library representative per room
Format
Seven parallel breakout rooms, each with a leader, an instructions monitor, a timekeeper, and testers

Written for August 2025. Several rooms assume a paid ChatGPT account, which was the state of the tools then; some of what needed a subscription in these instructions no longer does, and the interfaces have moved. The workflows still hold.

What the session was for

Putting it into practice

  • Humans stay in charge — AI never works alone
  • You are the senior editor, and you hold final approval
  • Bring your own context and expertise to guide the work
  • Use the right tool — AI is one option, not always the answer

Learning by doing

  • Turn curiosity into action: try the tools on something that actually matters to you.
  • Start small, finish something, reflect, improve. Then raise the bar.
  • Notice the pattern — goal, context, example, feedback, revision — and repeat it until the result sings.
  • Carry the wins forward. Each round builds judgment, speed, and imagination.

Keeping going afterward

  • Open the trained chat for a room, add your own materials, and keep working in it.
  • Follow the text instructions and the Drive folder for the labs and templates.
  • Save what works. Keep a personal prompt library and notes on what each one produced.

7

Room seven · Facilitator: Taryn Marks

Choose your own adventure: GenAI 101

The room for anyone who was not sure where to start, or who wanted to talk about AI generally rather than build something. No tools required, no prerequisites, and the discussion went where the questions went.

The questions on the table:

  • How do I know when to use generative AI — and when not to?
  • What does critical thinking look like alongside these tools?
  • What is Perplexity, and how is it different from Google?
  • What process should I follow to decide whether to use AI at all?

That last question became the PAUSE Rule, and this room’s ground is now covered by Introduction to AI for Beginners.

Privacy and confidentiality. These rooms have you upload your own work to third-party tools. Check how each one treats your data first, keep confidential and client material out, and apply the PAUSE Rule before you start.
